Leadership

Building high-impact university partnerships

A partnership succeeds when both sides can name what they gain. In my work with universities, the strongest ones started with a shared outcome, such as employable graduates or stronger enrolment, instead of a product list. From there, delivery discipline matters: clear service levels, regular reviews with the people who run the programme day to day, and honest reporting when something slips. Trust is built in operations, not in the signing ceremony.

Digital transformation

What a $5M transformation project taught me about admissions

Technology alone does not lift enrolment. When we led a digital transformation for a private university, the admissions gains came from joining systems, processes and people: a smoother applicant journey, dependable infrastructure and staff who were trained to use it. Over two years admissions rose by 40%. My lesson: fund the change management with the same seriousness as the hardware.

IT education

The future of IT education in Nigeria

Employers want people who can configure a network, secure a system and explain the business case. That means curricula built with industry certifications and real labs, taught by people who have worked in the field. After a decade of teaching at Nigerian universities and later leading training businesses, I believe the best programmes pair academic depth with practical, certifiable skill.

Analytics

Data analytics for better business decisions

Good analytics begins with a decision, not a dashboard. Ask what choice the numbers should inform, find the smallest dataset that answers it, and agree in advance what result would change your mind. In sales and marketing this keeps teams focused on the few measures that actually move revenue.
